From vectors to evidence

A query is a calculation. Its result is empirical.

Vector arithmetic defines a direction to search. Which word lies nearest depends on the learned vectors, the candidate vocabulary, and the scoring rule. An analogy such as king − man + woman proposes a query; it does not establish a guaranteed identity.

The two-dimensional word vectors in this lesson are invented to make the calculations visible. Their neighbors are computed exactly from those numbers. They are not results from the Google News model. The final map is an actual t-SNE fit to a separate, explicitly synthetic set of 18 six-dimensional vectors. Even here, neighborhoods are only approximate: A5’s three nearest source neighbors are A4, A6, and A1; on the map they are A4, A6, and A3.

Pretrained Google News vectors let us query representations learned from a large text corpus. Querying the stored vectors does not retrain the input and output matrices. This lesson follows its named-word query convention: normalize each source vector before signed addition. Its raw-vector query instead adds the stored vectors before comparing directions. Named-word inputs are excluded from their own result lists; a raw vector carries no word identities to exclude.

Before mapping a subset, check that each token exists, preserve capitalization, and remove duplicate entries. Filtering a list of 100 words can leave fewer than 100. t-SNE perplexity must be smaller than the number of points. The choice of words, perplexity, and initialization can all change the map.
