Partially linear DML

Use flexible predictions to remove covariate patterns while preserving a low-dimensional causal parameter.

The constructed example uses information-exposure intensity D, a continuous treatment, and a political-knowledge score Y. X is a centered engagement index. Fifty points exhaust a balanced grid of engagement, treatment disturbances, and outcome disturbances. The first calculations use known population nuisance functions to isolate the geometry.

9 / 13 · Orthogonality

Small errors have a second-order effect.

Near the true nuisance functions, the response is locally flat. Orthogonality removes first-order sensitivity of the population score.

∂t E[ψ(W;θ0,η0+th)]∣t=0=0\left.\partial_t\,\mathbb E[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0+t h)]\right|_{t=0}=0∂t​E[ψ(W;θ0​,η0​+th)]∣t=0​=0

For this example the fitted slope is an even function of ttt, so its first derivative at zero vanishes. In the displayed condition, θ0\theta_0θ0​ and η0\eta_0η0​ denote the true parameter and nuisance functions. The formal orthogonality condition concerns the population score, ψ=D~(Y~−θD~)\psi=\widetilde D(\widetilde Y-\theta\widetilde D)ψ=D(Y−θD). It does not say that each observation's score remains unchanged. Nor is it AIPW's global claim of consistency when either nuisance specification is correct: the partialling-out score can be biased when only the outcome conditional mean is correct.

10 / 13 · Cross-fit

Keep fitting and scoring separate.

Fit the outcome and treatment prediction models on the other folds. Use them to form residuals in the held-out fold.

Y~i=Yi−ℓ^−k(i)(Xi),D~i=Di−m^−k(i)(Xi)\widetilde Y_i=Y_i-\widehat\ell_{-k(i)}(X_i),\quad\widetilde D_i=D_i-\widehat m_{-k(i)}(X_i)Yi​=Yi​−ℓ−k(i)​(Xi​),Di​=Di​−m−k(i)​(Xi​)

Cross-fitting stops an observation's outcome and treatment from entering its own nuisance training data. Tuning and preprocessing also belong inside the training portion. Orthogonality addresses first-order sensitivity; cross-fitting addresses dependence from reusing the same observations in nuisance fitting and scoring. This fold diagram is schematic. It does not claim to train nuisance models in the browser.

11 / 13 · Cross-fit

Pool the held-out residual products.

After every fold has been held out, combine all numerator contributions and all denominator contributions. Then take their ratio.

θ^=∑k∑i∈IkD~iY~i∑k∑i∈IkD~i2\widehat\theta=\frac{\sum_k\sum_{i\in I_k}\widetilde D_i\widetilde Y_i}{\sum_k\sum_{i\in I_k}\widetilde D_i^2}θ=∑k​∑i∈Ik​​Di2​∑k​∑i∈Ik​​Di​Yi​​

The displayed estimator pools the residual products across all observations. It does not silently average fold-specific slopes. Folds with different treatment-residual variation contribute differently to the pooled ratio. Repeating sample splits can assess sensitivity to the split, but cross-fitting alone does not establish nuisance accuracy or valid inference.

Every observation contributes once as a held-out observation.

13 / 13 · Interpret the target

A constant coefficient is a restriction.

If the treatment effect varies, a single residual slope need not be the population average treatment effect.

θPLR=E[e(X)(1−e(X))τ(X)]E[e(X)(1−e(X))]\theta_{\mathrm{PLR}}=\frac{\mathbb E[e(X)(1-e(X))\tau(X)]}{\mathbb E[e(X)(1-e(X))]}θPLR​=E[e(X)(1−e(X))]E[e(X)(1−e(X))τ(X)]​

For binary treatment under conditional exchangeability, the population residual slope weights the conditional treatment effect by e(X)(1−e(X))e(X)(1-e(X))e(X)(1−e(X)). This last scene switches to a binary-treatment example to make that distinction visible. Two equally common groups have effects 6 and 2, and propensities 0.5 and 0.1. Their ordinary ATE is 4, while the residual slope is approximately 4.94. Choosing a target precedes choosing an estimator.
