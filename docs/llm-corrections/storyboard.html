The proposed sequence

  1. Introduce the measurement problem and the random audit.
  2. LLM-only analysis: predict, audit, assess, plug in.
  3. Classical supervised learning: label, train, validate, plug in.
  4. DSL: predict, randomly audit, optionally improve, correct.
  5. Correct a population average using the audit’s average error.
  6. Keep the average unchanged while changing the regression slope.
  7. Reveal the 16 expert-coded posts available to the estimator.
  8. Correct the three moments entering the regression coefficient.
  9. Compare the resulting slopes against the full-information benchmark.
  10. Estimate population standard errors and distinguish them from audit-only variation.

The examples are constructed for teaching. The support-share example and the 40-post regression example are separate datasets. The full-information expert values are available only to show the benchmark; the correction uses the randomly audited observations. The final lesson will retain the notebook’s source data and results.

Source: Egami, Hinck, Stewart, and Wei, Using Large Language Model Annotations for the Social Sciences: A General Framework of Using Predicted Variables in Downstream Analyses. The procedure lists are paraphrases. This introductory specialization keeps predictions fixed and audit probabilities known. The general doubly robust theory and learned prediction models are deferred.
